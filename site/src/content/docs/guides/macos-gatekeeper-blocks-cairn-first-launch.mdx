macOS blocks the first launch of `Cairn.app` because the desktop app is signed with Cairn's fixed self-signed certificate rather than Apple notarization yet. To open Cairn safely without stripping permissions on future updates, drag `Cairn.app` to `/Applications`, open **System Settings -> Privacy & Security**, and click **Open Anyway** next to Cairn.

## Exact steps by macOS version

1. **Move to `/Applications` first:** Do not launch directly from the mounted DMG volume or Downloads (running from a translocated path also disables in-app updates).
2. **macOS 15 Sequoia and newer:** Double-click **Cairn** in `/Applications`, dismiss the Gatekeeper dialog, open **System Settings -> Privacy & Security**, scroll down to the Security section, and click **Open Anyway**.
3. **macOS 14 Sonoma and earlier:** Right-click (Control-click) **Cairn.app** in `/Applications` and choose **Open** from the context menu, then confirm **Open** in the dialog.

## Why Cairn uses a fixed self-signed certificate

Instead of ad-hoc signing (`identity: '-'`), Cairn signs releases with a consistent certificate so macOS preserves your Accessibility and Screen Recording permissions across in-app auto-updates. When you click **Update to …** in the sidebar later, Cairn verifies the downloaded `.zip` SHA-256 and checks the certificate before swapping the bundle.
