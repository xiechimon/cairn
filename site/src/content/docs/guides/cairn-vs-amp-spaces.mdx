Cairn and **Amp Spaces** share a similar thread-card mental model—chatting in one place while background threads execute tasks in parallel—but they serve opposite audiences. **Cairn** is a free, Apache-2.0 personal assistant for **one developer** running on their own Mac and hardware; **Amp Spaces** is a multiplayer workspace built for **engineering teams**.

## Feature comparison (checked 2026-11-27)

| Dimension | Cairn | Amp Spaces |
|---|---|---|
| **Target audience** | One solo developer (each person runs their own copy) | Teams sharing spaces, threads, and goals ([Amp Spaces docs](https://ampcode.com/docs/spaces)) |
| **Hosting & data** | Local Mac desktop app or self-hosted server (`server.db` + `secretbox.key`) | Hosted Amp workspace with cloud/orb threads |
| **Billing & models** | $0 from Cairn; BYO ChatGPT, Claude Code, Gemini, or custom endpoints | Workspace billing and Amp model selection |
| **Slack / IM integration** | Personal outbound Telegram, Slack DM Socket Mode, WeChat iLink | Team Slack channel `@Amp` integration inside shared Spaces |
| **Code storage** | Local folders or GitHub repos on your own Runners | Amp project repositories & workspace threads |

## Where Amp Spaces is better

Amp Spaces is clearly the right choice when multiple teammates need to watch the same live thread cards, collaborate in shared `@Amp` Slack channels, share workspace-level instructions, and bill usage to a single team account without managing local Runners or Tailscale.

## When Cairn fits better

Choose Cairn when you are building solo, already pay for ChatGPT or Claude Code, and want full Apache-2.0 ownership with your code, SQLite state, and keys never leaving your own Mac or servers ([Cairn for solo developers](/guides/cairn-for-solo-developers-vs-teams/)).
